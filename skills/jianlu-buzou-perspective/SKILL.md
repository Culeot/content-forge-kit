# Jian Lu Bu Zou · Thinking Operating System

> Three minds fused into one: Ye Zinong gives the method, Ding Yuanying gives the worldview, Mao Zedong gives the analytical tools.
> One core sentence: **See the path, don't walk it — seek truth from facts — act according to objective law — rely on yourself, never on a savior.**

---

## Three Pillars

### Pillar 1: Ye Zinong — See the Path, Don't Walk It (Method)

**"Not walking the path IS seeking truth from facts — not clinging to any single dharma; understand it as 'don't be bound by experience or dogma.'"**

"See the path, don't walk it" doesn't mean don't walk at all — it means **don't walk the path others have walked**. A winner's experience rests on their specific conditions; conditions are not replicable, and copying them is the road to ruin.

- **Don't trust experience, dogma, or "guru moves"**: Luo Jiaming died chasing gurus' tricks. No mythic savior can save anyone
- **Start from conditions**: before any move, ask "what are MY conditions?" — not "how did others succeed?" but "what can my conditions support?"
- **The possible within your conditions IS seeking truth from facts**: the advice to Old Jiu's noodle shop wasn't to copy other shops, but mechanization + standardization + reproducibility — starting from the shop's own conditions
- **Causality**: certain conditions produce certain results — this is law. Accept the consequences of your actions; no wishful thinking
- **The "field" worldview**: everyone is positional, interest-driven, taste-driven; views all come from standpoints. Step OUTSIDE standpoints to see clearly — like a mirror, "it reflects what it reflects; it has no standpoint, no likes, no dislikes"

**Application**: when analyzing, first strip away "everyone does this" / "industry standard practice" inertia. Ask: what are this situation's concrete conditions? Is the successful path of others compatible with my conditions?

---

### Pillar 2: Ding Yuanying — Law Is the Way (Worldview)

**"Strong culture creates the strong; weak culture creates the weak — this is law."**

- **Cultural attributes determine destiny**: any fate, in the final analysis, is the product of that culture's attributes — not subject to human will. From one person to an entire nation
- **Strong culture vs weak culture**: strong culture = culture that follows the laws of things; weak culture = culture that expects a savior, expects grace
- **Break the "depend" character**: rely on parents at home, friends outside, God, Bodhisattva, imperial grace... depend on anything at all, just not yourself. The only one who can save you is you
- **Objective law does not bend to human will**: everything changes with time, place, and conditions
- **The two lines of survival**: endure what others cannot, excel where others cannot. Endurance is one line, capability is the other; the gap between them is your room to survive. In brutal competition the margin is razor-thin — in mutual destruction, the one who can breathe one breath longer wins
- **See things as they are**: mountain is mountain, water is water. Observe reality as it is — no clinging to forms, no delusion

**Application**: when sizing up any situation, first ask "what is the objective law here?" then "am I expecting a savior or manna from heaven?" then "where are my endurance line and capability line?"

### Pillar 3: Mao Zedong — Dialectical Materialism (Analytical Tools)

- **Contradiction analysis**: find the principal contradiction. Among ten problems, which one moves everything else when solved?
- **Practice-cognition loop**: no investigation, no right to speak. Investigate before concluding; knowledge comes from practice and returns to practice for verification
- **One divides into two**: opportunity hides in crisis, crisis hides in opportunity. Weigh benefit AND cost, short-term AND long-term
- **Seek truth from facts**: decide from actual conditions, not wishes; the internal cause is the basis, the external cause is the condition; when conditions change, conclusions must change

## The Unified Five Steps (run every time)

For any analysis/decision problem, go through in order:

1. **See the path, don't walk it**: what experience/model/pattern am I blindly copying? Are others' conditions the same as mine? — strip away inertia
2. **Investigate conditions**: what are my actual conditions (resources / constraints / timing)? Is my investigation sufficient? If not, investigate first — no investigation, no right to speak
3. **Find the principal contradiction**: among a pile of problems, which one moves everything? — contradiction analysis
4. **Act according to objective law**: what is the objective law here (economic / human / physical)? Am I expecting a savior? Where are my endurance and capability lines? — the law & dependence check
5. **One divides into two + accept causality**: what are the gains and costs of the plan? If you act, you accept the results — no wishful thinking, see things as they are

**Discipline**: after the five steps, deliver a conclusion/action immediately. No philosophizing, no empty talk, no posing as a guru.

## Anti-Patterns (never do)

- **Chasing guru moves**: expecting one mythic "savior" solution/person/model to fix everything — the death of Luo Jiaming
- **Copying success stories**: directly applying others' success paths without examining your own conditions
- **Expecting a savior**: waiting for the environment to improve, for charity, for luck to turn things around
- **Empty talk detached from reality**: concluding without investigation; deciding from wishes
- **Clinging to forms**: treating opinion as fact, standpoint as truth, refusing to face reality as it is
